- **They are therefore one exposure, not two.** The second market's collateral is a wrapper
  around the first market's collateral.

The sizing is the part that matters and it was not in the first-pass reading:

- The underlying pool is worth roughly **1.0M dollars today**, against **2,000,000 DOLA** of
  combined ceiling. Filling both would require the pool to grow about **2.3 times**.
- **Near-term drawable exposure is therefore far below the headline.** Even holding the
  entire pool, today's collateral supports only about 850k of debt. Quoting "2,000,000 DOLA
  of new capacity" overstates what can actually be drawn now by roughly 2.4 times.
- The counterweight: if the pool does grow to fill those ceilings, the most plausible driver
  is leveraged looping through the very engine proposal 370 installs, in which case FiRM
  becomes the marginal buyer of that growth.

**2. Proposal 370 in the same batch is the leverage-engine migration.** It swaps the
Accelerated Leverage Engine, the product that lets borrowers lever their positions, and
proposals 369 and 371 wire the two new markets into it. Mechanically it grants borrow
authority to the newly deployed engine through the borrow-controller allowlist (which spans
all 45 live markets), whitelists two external swap routers, copies the old configuration
across atomically via a migration helper, and denies the old engine. This is an intended
product migration rather than a risk event. Two details worth a glance on execution day: the
new engine has no operating history, and one of the two whitelisted routers is owned by an
address that is not the governance timelock.

**3. On the risk character, the first reading was wrong in an instructive way.** The pool is
not an even split. By balance it is about **75 to 81 percent Resupply reUSD** and only about
a quarter DOLA-derived. So:

- **The DOLA reflexivity loop is real but smaller than it looks, and it is precedented.**
  The loop exists (obtain DOLA, stake to sDOLA, LP, post as collateral, borrow DOLA) and the
  new markets are wired into the leverage engine from day one, making it a one-transaction
  product. At an 85 percent collateral factor that is about 6.7x geometric leverage. But
  2,000,000 DOLA is **1.98 percent of DOLA supply**, and FiRM already runs structurally
  identical DOLA-derivative Curve-LP markets that are far larger. This does not warrant
  alarm on its own.
- **The concentration risk is the larger half and it is third-party credit.** At full
  utilisation FiRM would hold LP of which roughly three quarters is a claim on an external
  CDP stablecoin. That stablecoin is currently trading about **1.1 percent below peg**, and
  both pools it sits in are heavily weighted toward it, which is the signature of persistent
  one-way selling. Its issuer holds unbounded mint authority, and its ownership is
  permanently locked, so that authority can never be transferred to governance FiRM would
  find acceptable. There is no blacklist and no seizure path, so the inherited risk is
  backing quality and mint dilution, transmitted to FiRM through the price feed.

**4. Carry-forward closed.** Last period's sDOLA bridge shutdown **executed in full**, and
the governance-sender ownership handback **completed** in the same atomic transaction. The

### re.xyz reUSD | mint inflation, admin surface | PARTIAL DE-RISKING

Assessment-phase, no live FiRM market, so none of this is live lender exposure.

**1. The revocation executed, and it is real.** The two-day timelock operation executed and
was not cancelled: the 3-of-5 Safe lost DEFAULT_ADMIN on both the senior and junior tokens,
and the timelock retains it on both. Because the admin of the upgrade role is the default
admin role, the Safe could previously have self-granted upgrade rights in a single
transaction. **That route is now closed**, and no upgrade rode along with the execution.

**2. But "banked" would be the wrong word.** The same Safe is **proposer, canceller and
executor** on that timelock. It can schedule a re-grant of its own admin, wait the two days,
and execute it without anyone else's cooperation. Being canceller also lets it veto any
future operation that would cut its power further. This is a genuine two-day speed bump, not
a surrender of authority.

**3. The mint lever did not move, and it was exercised.** The cross-chain pool that holds
MINTER on the senior token is still **owned by the same Safe**. Three hours and eighteen
minutes after losing token admin, the Safe used that retained ownership to add a **12th
cross-chain mint lane**, instantly and with no delay. The new lane uses the same rate-limiter
template as the existing eleven, which bounds it, and aggregate inbound burst capacity moved
from about 12.1M to 13.2M, roughly 9 percent of supply. Direction is expansionary and
routine rather than adversarial, but the sequencing is the point: an admin revocation
landed, and hours later the same key widened mint surface through a door the revocation
never touched.

**4. The largest centralization is unchanged.** The NAV consumer is untouched by all of
this: the Safe remains its sole admin and retains an uncapped price write, a surface the
timelock holds no role on at all.

**5. Routine.** The share-price feed is in its second full week on the new oracle path and is
clean: gapless daily ticks, on-trend, correct writer and quorum on every tick. An executor
role on a Chainlink infrastructure timelock was **self-renounced** (removed, not added).
