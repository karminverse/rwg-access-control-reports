## Changed (6)

### frxUSD | mint inflation, proxy upgrade | WATCH

- **What it is for, in plain terms.** frxUSD is redeemable against reserves held through a set of approved custodian contracts, each accepting one asset and minting frxUSD against it. Until this week only the USDC custodian was live on chain. The new one accepts **AUSD**, Agora's dollar stablecoin, whose reserves sit in a Delaware statutory trust with VanEck managing the fund and State Street providing custody. Frax's published custodian roster also names Circle, Securitize, Superstate, Centrifuge and WisdomTree assets, each authorised by its own governance proposal. This one is **FIP-425, "AUSD as Collateral and Custodian Asset for frxUSD", which proposed an initial 25,000,000 cap**. Read that way, the deployment is reserve diversification under a documented programme, not a novel mint rail, and the 100,000 first tranche is consistent with a pilot.
  Two sourcing caveats, stated so they are not over-read: we confirmed the backing token is AUSD directly on chain, but we did not verify the proposal's final vote outcome or reconcile its dates against the 2026-08-07 deployment. The on-chain grant is consistent with approval; treat the governance chronology as indicative.

- **A second custodian was granted the frxUSD minter role** at block 25,832,426 on 2026-08-25. This is the only genuine authority movement in the frxUSD graph this week. The authorising path was a 4-of-4 signature batch on the usual 4-of-7 multisig, which owns the token.

- **It is an expansion, not a migration.** The incumbent custodian keeps its 400,000,000 mint cap byte-identical at both endpoints, is still a minter, and processed 2,211 events during the week. A rail was added alongside, not swapped in.

- **Nothing has been minted through it.** Zero assets held, zero issued, zero collateral deposited, both in the window and since its deployment on 2026-08-07. Realised exposure today is zero.

- **Supply fell and reconciles exactly.** Total supply went from 101,608,611.82 to 101,179,942.95, down 428,668.87. A full enumeration of every mint and burn in the window gives 13,824,516.56 minted against 14,253,185.43 burned, matching to the wei. No inflation.

- **The 100,000 cap is not an on-chain constraint on the role.** The token exposes no per-minter cap of any kind. Simulated from the new custodian, a mint of ten billion succeeds; the same call from a non-minter is rejected, and the same call from this address at the baseline block was rejected, so the capability is genuinely new. - **The new rail is the only contract in the frxUSD graph not controlled by the 4-of-7.** Every other proxy in that graph routes to one timelock with a 1-day delay owned by the 4-of-7. This one routes to a different, long-dormant timelock with a 2-day delay whose administrator is a 3-of-5 multisig. Twenty-four hours of extra delay were bought at the price of one fewer required signature, on the newest mint machinery.

- **No new individual signer entered.** The 3-of-5's five owners are a strict subset of the 4-of-7's seven. The point is not new people, it is a smaller quorum: any three of those five previously had no frxUSD authority at all and can now queue an implementation change, wait two days, and mint without a cap. Diff tooling reports no members entering because it enumerates individual accounts rather than multisigs.

- **The dormancy reassurance applies to the wrong contract.** The 2-day timelock has emitted nothing in roughly 166 days, which is true. Its administering multisig ran 12 transactions inside this window, two of them plain transfers of about 21,500 frxUSD each. The delay contract is cold; the quorum that administers it is not.

- **Three contracts are reported as added, but none was deployed this week.** All three already carried code at the baseline block, dating to 2020, 2021 and 2026-08-07. They entered the graph only because the minter grant made them reachable. The grant is real; three new deployments is not.

- **The dependency this introduces:** frxUSD's backing now has a path to AUSD, a third-party issuer's stablecoin currently around 64,000,000 in supply, sitting outside our roster and outside our scan scope. Exposure is zero today. If the tranche is raised toward the authorised 25,000,000, AUSD's own reserve and authority structure becomes a dependency worth assessing on its own terms.

## FiRM's pause guardian is one signature, and pausing is one-way

Not a change this week, and it is here because every verdict that mentioned this guardian cited it as a reassurance and none asked what it was.

The pause guardian on **all 47 FiRM markets** is a multisig with a **threshold of one** and four owners. Simulated at both window ends on all three markets holding roster collateral, a pause succeeds from the guardian and is rejected from an unrelated address. **Unpausing reverts for the guardian and is reachable only by governance**, meaning the 2-day timelock. So the forced-pause vector is the cheapest lever on the book: one signature of four, instant, no delay, and reversible only by a DAO vote. 31 of the 47 markets are already paused, so the lever is in routine use.

Nothing was paused this week: the guardian emitted no events and the timelock executed nothing. Worth noting for completeness that pausing emits no event at all, so this was established by reading state at both endpoints rather than by observing an absence of events.

- **Cross-scope levers are invisible by construction.** The Ethena timelock sits in one asset's scope while the functions it can reach without delay sit in another's, or in none.
